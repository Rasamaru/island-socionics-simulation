# Methodology and assumptions

## Purpose

Explore how four small groups might develop under broadly comparable survival conditions, while testing whether proposed differences between socionic quadras help explain any patterns in the model.

This is a scenario model, not an empirical test that can establish the validity of socionics.

## Evidence levels

Every record should distinguish:

- **Scenario fact** — explicitly established in the shared simulation.
- **Observation** — an action, statement, or outcome recorded in the narrative.
- **Interpretation** — a possible explanation of an observation.
- **Model assumption** — a rule introduced to make the simulation executable.
- **Estimate** — an uncertain quantity, with its basis and confidence stated.

Do not silently turn interpretations or assumptions into facts.

## Individual agents

Agents have individual biographies, skills, experience, preferences, limitations, and relationships. Type is only one possible explanatory variable and must not replace individual-level causes.

## Environment

The island has four regions with different terrain. All regions provide access to freshwater, wood, stone, clay, edible plants, animal or fish resources, cultivable land, fibers, and other basic materials, but local abundance and accessibility may vary.

Environmental differences must not be so large that they predetermine the outcome.

## Uncertainty and realism

Account for weather, seasonality, injuries, illness, fatigue, learning, resource depletion, failed attempts, and random events. Small populations have limited labor, specialist coverage, medical capacity, and demographic resilience.

## Reporting

For each simulated period, report:
- what happened;
- which agents were involved (name and type);
- immediate and delayed consequences;
- resource and population state where available;
- uncertainty and competing explanations.

Never infer a type from one isolated action, nor infer a causal effect from a single simulated outcome.
